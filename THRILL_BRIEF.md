# THRILL BRIEF — The game in the founder’s head  
### Boss must read this before every decision. If a feature doesn’t serve this, cut it.

---

## One-sentence product

**People put attention into a shared pot (by watching ads). When enough tickets are in, the world holds its breath for a number. If it’s yours, you take the pot. Everything else is seasoning.**

---

## The feeling (what they imagined before we coded)

Not “an app with rooms and upgrades.”

### 1. I bought a shot with my time
I watch an ad (or three). That’s not a loading screen — that’s **me putting chips on the table**.  
More ads = more tickets = I *chose* to lean in. If I’m alone and I fill the room myself, I *earned* the whole pot. Destiny, not charity.

### 2. The room is a living tension
N is filling. 3/10… 7/10… **almost**.  
Even if I leave the app, the room still exists. Almost full. Push: “One seat left.”  
The thrill is **waiting with other people for the same moment** — shared suspense, not solo busywork.

### 3. The draw is the climax (not a UI transition)
When the room fills, time slows.  
Not a spinner for decoration — a **ceremony**:
- The pot is big and clear  
- My tickets are *mine* and glowing  
- Odds are real (“2 of 10 — I have a real shot”)  
- Near-miss on my number should *hurt in a good way*  
- Hit should feel like winning the lottery for a second  

**If the draw is weak, the product is dead.** That is the fun part. Fight, upgrades, guilds are aftershocks.

### 4. Winning means something
Even before real cash: the pot should feel like **wealth / score / status**, not a dead counter.  
Coins were always the **jackpot currency** — the thing the draw is *for*.  
Gems / fights / upgrades were the **second loop**: “I also get stronger / cooler when I’m not the lucky one.”

### 5. Two crowns (optional spice, not the main course)
- **Pot King** = pure luck (the original idea)  
- **Arena Champ** = my fighter / build (so non-winners of the pot still have a story)  

Same match, maybe different people. Double crown is legendary.  
But: **if we only have energy for one spectacle, it is the draw.**

### 6. I grow into a killer (slow burn)
Start bare — medieval nobody.  
Upgrades change how I *look* and hit.  
That’s identity and long-term hope — **not** the first 30 seconds.

### 7. Later: real money + teams
Cash-out when legal.  
Guilds, split pots, PvP gem rooms — **scale layers**, not day-one noise.

---

## Emotional beats (in order)

```
Want a shot  →  watch ad / buy ticket
See room fill →  tension / FOMO / almost
DRAW         →  peak (this is the product)
Win or near-miss →  need another ticket
(Fight / grow / flex) →  keep me if luck failed
Come back when push says “full” or “almost”
```

The player should leave thinking:  
**“One more ad. One more ticket. That almost hit.”**

Not:  
“I upgraded footwork and watched bots trade HP.”

---

## What we must never do (kills the original feel)

| Kill shot | Why |
|-----------|-----|
| Draw as a 2-second spinner afterthought | Deletes the product |
| Coins that do nothing forever | Jackpot fantasy dies |
| Instant auto-fill so there’s no “waiting with others” | Kills social tension (bots OK as scaffold, but room must *feel* like a pit filling) |
| Fight longer / louder than the draw | Wrong climax |
| Day-one unlock trees, create caps, PvP stakes in the face | Dilutes the pot ritual |
| SaaS lobby with equal buttons | No “I’m in a game show” |

---

## Non-negotiable for “we live it”

Boss: a session is a **success** only if:

1. **Ticket purchase feels like commitment** (ad or clear cost)  
2. **Fill state is visible and tense** (even with House help)  
3. **Draw is the longest, loudest, most personal moment** in the match  
4. **Player can name their tickets** before the draw ends  
5. **Near-miss and hit are both memorable**  
6. **Jackpot currency (coins) has a reason to exist** (spend, flex, or clear “this becomes cash”)  
7. Fight/upgrade never block or outshine the draw on first sessions  

---

## Success quote (use as north star)

> “I watched the ads, I held #3 and #7, the room filled, the show started, #7 almost landed, then #4 hit — I need one more ticket.”

If the build doesn’t produce that sentence, **we are not building their game.**
